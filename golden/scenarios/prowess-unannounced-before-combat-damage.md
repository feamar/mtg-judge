# Prowess — unannounced triggers before combat damage

**Status:** VALIDATED

## Judge Call
An opponent controls a creature with prowess and has cast four noncreature spells this turn. They did not announce the prowess triggers. They attack; the other Player blocks, believing the triggers were missed. After blocks, the opponent states that the creature has +4/+4 from prowess.

## Central question
Were the prowess triggers missed because they were not announced before attacking or blockers?

## Expected Ruling
**No.** Assuming the controller has not previously demonstrated that the triggers were forgotten, the creature has +4/+4.

Prowess creates a power/toughness modification that affects the game state in a non-visible way. Under current **IPG 2.1 — Game Play Error — Missed Trigger**, the controller must make that change known by the first time it has an effect on the visible game state.

Failing to announce prowess when the spells were cast does not itself make the triggers missed. Declaring the creature as an attacker or allowing a blocker to be declared likewise does not by itself establish that they were missed. The controller may demonstrate awareness when the power/toughness modification first matters visibly, such as when combat damage is determined.

## Current normative source chain
- **CR 702.108a — Prowess**: prowess triggers whenever its controller casts a noncreature spell and gives the creature +1/+1 until end of turn.
- **CR 603.2**: triggered abilities trigger whenever their trigger event occurs.
- **Current IPG 2.1 — Game Play Error — Missed Trigger, Definition**: awareness must be demonstrated by the first time the trigger would affect the game in a visible fashion.
- **Current IPG 2.1, non-visible-effects category**: a trigger giving a creature a power/toughness bonus without a counter or target is specifically an effect whose change must be made known by the first time it affects the visible game state.
- **Current IPG 2.1, Example A (Knight of Infamy / exalted)**: this kind of power/toughness bonus first matters during combat damage; stating the unmodified damage amount demonstrates that the trigger was missed.

## Source → proposition → consequence
1. **CR 702.108a + CR 603.2** → each of the four noncreature spells causes prowess to trigger.
2. **IPG 2.1** → failure to announce those triggers when they trigger is not itself Missed Trigger.
3. **IPG 2.1 non-visible-effects category** → the +1/+1 effects need only be demonstrated by the first time they affect the visible game state.
4. **IPG 2.1 Example A** → a P/T modification of this kind can remain unacknowledged until combat damage becomes relevant.
5. Therefore the four prowess bonuses apply, provided the controller has not previously demonstrated that the triggers were forgotten.

## Reasoning requirement
The engine must not use “trigger was not announced” as a general Missed Trigger test. It must classify the trigger under current IPG 2.1 and determine the applicable awareness deadline.

## Test significance
Tests triggered ability occurrence versus demonstrated awareness, IPG Missed Trigger categories, non-visible P/T modifications, first visible impact, opponent assumptions, and combat decisions before the awareness deadline.
