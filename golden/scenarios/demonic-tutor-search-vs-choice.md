# Demonic Tutor — search decision is not a CR 608.2d choice

**Status:** VALIDATED

## Judge Call
A Player asks what choices are made during resolution. Demonic Tutor is raised as an example.

## Central question
When resolving **Demonic Tutor**, is determining which card to find a “choice” governed by **CR 608.2d**?

## Expected Ruling
**No.**

Demonic Tutor instructs its controller to **search** their library for a card. This invokes **CR 701.23**. The fact that the Player determines which card to find does not by itself make that determination a rules-defined choice under CR 608.2d.

CR 608.2d concerns choices offered by an effect during resolution and states that those choices are **announced** when the effect is applied unless made earlier. A Demonic Tutor search does not require the Player to announce the identity of the card being found.

Therefore, “a Player makes a decision during resolution” is not equivalent to “the Player makes a choice under CR 608.2d.”

## Current-CR normative source chain
- **CR 608.2d** — choices offered during resolution are announced when the effect is applied unless already made earlier.
- **CR 701.23a** — defines searching for a card in a zone and finding a card matching the stated description.
- **CR 701.23d** — covers hidden-zone searches simply for a quantity of cards; Demonic Tutor searches for “a card.”
- **Current Oracle text — Demonic Tutor** — instructs the Player to search their library for a card, put it into their hand, then shuffle.

## Source → proposition → consequence
1. **Demonic Tutor Oracle text** → instructs a Search; it does not instruct an announced choice of card.
2. **CR 701.23a / 701.23d** → the Player performs the defined Search action and finds a card satisfying the instruction.
3. **CR 608.2d** → rules-defined choices made during resolution are announced.
4. Demonic Tutor does not require announcement of the identity of the card found → finding that card as part of Search is not a CR 608.2d choice.
5. An engine must therefore not classify every decision made during resolution as a “choice.”

## Reasoning requirement
The engine must distinguish a Player decision made while carrying out an instruction, a formal choice governed by rules such as CR 608.2d, and a defined game action such as Search whose procedure requires determining an object.

## Test significance
This is a counterexample to the heuristic: “Every decision made by a Player during resolution is a choice under CR 608.2d.”
