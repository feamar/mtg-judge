# Priority

## Status
Working canonical concept model derived from the Magic: The Gathering Comprehensive Rules. The Comprehensive Rules remain normative if this model is incomplete or inaccurate.

## Canonical minimal player-facing explanation

### English
During a game, priority is just who gets to act right now. You can cast a spell or activate an ability, or you can pass. If you pass, priority moves to the next player. If all players pass in a row, then the top item on the stack resolves. After that, the active player gets priority again. If all players pass in a row while the stack is empty, the game moves on to the next step or phase.

### Dutch
Tijdens een game is priority simpelweg wie er op dit moment iets mag doen. Je kunt een spell casten of een ability activeren, of je kunt passen. Als je past, gaat priority naar de volgende player. Als alle players achter elkaar passen, dan resolvet het bovenste item op de stack. Daarna krijgt de active player weer priority. Als alle players achter elkaar passen terwijl de stack leeg is, dan gaat de game door naar de volgende step of phase.

## Core concept model
- At ordinary priority moments, exactly one player has priority.
- A player with priority may take actions allowed at that time, including casting spells and activating abilities when applicable, or may pass.
- Passing moves priority according to the turn order/rules governing priority.
- If all players pass in succession while the stack is not empty, the top object on the stack resolves.
- After resolution and the required rules processing before priority is granted, the active player receives priority.
- If all players pass in succession while the stack is empty, the current step or phase ends.
- The stack becoming empty is not by itself the same event as all players passing in succession while the stack is empty.

## Casting without priority
Having priority is not a necessary condition for casting a spell when a resolving spell or ability instructs or permits a player to cast that spell.

A resolving effect can create a permission to cast a spell during its own resolution. In that case:
- the player does not receive priority merely in order to make that cast;
- the spell is cast during the resolution of the resolving object;
- the spell is put onto the stack normally;
- the resolving object remains on the stack beneath spells cast this way until it finishes resolving;
- no player receives priority between those casts merely because a spell has been put onto the stack;
- normal card-type timing permissions do not prevent the cast when the effect itself provides the relevant timing permission, though other applicable restrictions can still matter.

## Reasoning implication
The system must not equate “may cast a spell” with “has priority.” It must determine the source of permission to cast and whether an effect is allowing or instructing the cast during resolution.

## Language rule
Terms and definitions originating from the Comprehensive Rules are not translated in project interaction. They remain in English even when the surrounding explanation is in Dutch or another language.

## Normative references
Use the current Comprehensive Rules, especially the rules governing priority, the stack, resolving spells and abilities, and casting spells. The CR controls over this reverse-engineered concept model.
